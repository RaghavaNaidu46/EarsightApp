Earsight is an aid, not a replacement. Keep using your cane or guide dog. Earsight cannot see everything — glass doors, overhanging branches, fast-moving traffic and sudden drop-offs can all be missed. Never rely on it alone to decide whether it is safe to step or cross.

Using Earsight

Hang the phone on a lanyard or chest mount with the rear camera facing the direction you walk. That leaves both hands free. Then tap anywhere on the screen to start, and anywhere again to stop. You do not need to find a button — the whole screen is the button, and a short vibration confirms each change.

While scanning, Earsight speaks what it finds. It deliberately says very little. Speech is slow, and a busy street produces far more than anyone could listen to, so Earsight picks the single most important thing at a time and drops the rest. Hazards interrupt whatever is being said; everything else waits its turn. The result is quieter than you might expect, and that is on purpose — you still need to hear the street itself.

What it tells you

Steps down and kerbs
Highest priority. Announced as “step down” with the direction.
Obstacles
Anything solid in your path. Very close obstacles are announced as “stop”.
People and animals
With direction, and distance when your phone can measure it.
Signs and room numbers
Short signage is read aloud. Long text such as posters and menus is ignored on purpose.

The proximity tone

Alongside speech you will hear a tick that gets faster and higher as you close in on something, like a reversing sensor. It comes from the left or right to tell you which side. This carries distance continuously, without spending words on numbers that would be out of date by the time the sentence finished. You can turn it off in Settings.

What you need

  • An iPhone or iPad running iOS 18 or later.
  • For spoken distances, a device with a LiDAR sensor — iPhone 12 Pro and later Pro models, or a recent iPad Pro. Without LiDAR, Earsight still reads signs and spots people, but it will not guess distances it cannot measure, and it says so when it starts.
  • Camera permission. Earsight asks once, on first use.

Settings

How much to say
Essential — hazards and people only, the quietest option. Normal — adds signs. This is the default. Detailed — everything, including more distant things.
Distances in
Metres or feet. Set from your region the first time, changeable any time.
Speaking speed
Most experienced screen reader users prefer this faster than the default.
Proximity tone
Turns the closing-distance tick on or off.

Troubleshooting

It is not saying anything

Long silences are normal — Earsight stays quiet when there is nothing worth saying. If it never speaks, check that scanning is actually on (tap the screen and feel for the vibration), that your volume is up, and that the camera is not covered by a coat or bag strap.

It says it cannot measure distance

Your device has no LiDAR sensor. Signs and people still work. This is a hardware limit and cannot be fixed in software — estimating distance without a sensor would mean speaking numbers that might be wrong, which is worse than saying nothing.

It is warning me the phone is hot

Continuous camera, detection and speech is demanding work. When the phone gets warm, Earsight slows down naming things but keeps hazard detection at full speed. On long walks, a battery pack helps. The screen also dims itself after about fifteen seconds of scanning to save power; touch it to bring it back.

It keeps saying the same thing

Each kind of thing has a waiting period before it can be announced again — longest for signs, shortest for steps down you are still approaching. If something repeats a lot, you are probably still walking towards it.

Report a problem or ask a question

Please open an issue on GitHub: github.com/RaghavaNaidu46/EarsightApp/issues. Describe what you were doing, what Earsight said or failed to say, and which iPhone you were using. Safety-related reports are looked at first.
