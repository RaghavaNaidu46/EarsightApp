Privacy Policy

Earsight · last updated 12 October 2026

Back to support
Earsight collects no data at all. No personal information, no images, no video, no audio, no analytics, no identifiers. There is nothing to opt out of, because nothing is gathered in the first place.

What happens to the camera feed

Earsight reads frames from the camera, works out what is in them, turns that into speech, and discards each frame immediately. Frames exist only in memory, for a fraction of a second, while they are being examined. Earsight never writes them to storage, never adds them to your photo library, and never keeps a recording of any kind. When you stop scanning, or leave the app, there is nothing left to delete.

Nothing leaves your device

All recognition happens on the iPhone itself, using Apple’s on-device vision and speech frameworks. Earsight contains no networking code and makes no network requests. It has no servers, no cloud processing, no third-party SDKs, no advertising libraries and no crash or analytics reporting. There is no account to create and nothing to sign in to.

What is stored on your device

Four settings — how much to say, metres or feet, speaking speed, and whether the proximity tone is on — plus a note of whether you have seen the introduction. These are kept in the app’s own private storage on your device, are never transmitted anywhere, and are removed if you delete the app.

Permissions

Earsight asks for camera access, which it needs to see the path ahead. It does not request your location, microphone, contacts, photo library, health data, Bluetooth, or any other permission. You can withdraw camera access at any time in Settings › Privacy & Security › Camera; Earsight will then tell you it cannot scan, and nothing else about your phone changes.

Children

Earsight is safe for users of any age. Since it collects no data whatsoever, it gathers nothing from children either, and it contains no advertising, no purchases and no external links inside the app.

Changes to this policy

If Earsight ever changes how it handles information, this page will be updated before that version is released, and the date at the top will change. Given that the app’s design goal is to hold on to nothing, any such change would be a significant one and would be described plainly here.
